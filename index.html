Developing novel imaging technologies guided by a rigorous understanding of image science

The IMPACT Lab in the Department of Radiology at the University of Pennsylvania develops CT and cone-beam CT technologies through end-to-end modeling of the imaging chain, from the physics of data acquisition to the diagnostic decisions that images ultimately serve.

Research

Research areas

Novel Imaging Techniques for Interventional Applications

Non-circular orbits on robotic C-arms for metal artifact reduction and image quality improvement, with automatic geometric calibration on clinical systems.

Spectral Imaging for Angiography and Diagnostic CT

New spectral detector designs and estimation algorithms that improve material quantification and motion robustness in angiography and diagnostic CT.

Deep Learning Image Formation

Generative methods for CT reconstruction, with rigorous frameworks for assessing and communicating the behavior of nonlinear, data-dependent algorithms.

Physics-Guided Radiomics Standardization

Theoretical modeling and recovery of radiomic features in the presence of blur and noise, enabling robust quantitative imaging biomarkers across scanners and protocols.

Image Quality & Task-Driven Imaging

Quantitative models of imaging performance that connect system design to the clinical task, enabling prospective optimization of acquisition and reconstruction.

Clinical Translation

Close collaboration with radiologists, neurosurgeons, and industry partners to bring new imaging methods from theory to first patient studies.

Principal Investigator

Grace J. Gang, PhD

Dr. Gang is an Associate Professor in the Department of Radiology at the University of Pennsylvania and Adjunct Professor of Biomedical Engineering at Johns Hopkins University. Her research centers on a quantitative understanding of how imaging systems perform — and on using that understanding to design better ones. She is a Fellow of the AAPM, recipient of the AAPM John S. Laughlin Early-Career Scientist Award and the Fully3D Women-in-Science Award, and Associate Editor of Medical Physics.
