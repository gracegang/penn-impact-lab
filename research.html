Research

Novel Imaging Techniques for Interventional Applications

Non-Circular Orbit Design for Interventional Cone-Beam CT

We develop novel acquisition and reconstruction techniques for interventional cone-beam CT, including task-driven imaging and non-circular source-detector orbits for metal artifact reduction. This work uses sampling analysis, reconstruction modeling, and phantom experiments to evaluate how orbit design affects image quality near challenging anatomy and high-attenuation objects.

  • Non-circular orbit design for metal artifact reduction
  • Task-driven acquisition and reconstruction for cone-beam CT
Selected Presentations
  • Non-circular CT orbit design for elimination of metal artifacts - SPIE Medical Imaging 2020
  • Task-driven acquisition and reconstruction in cone-beam CT - SPIE Medical Imaging 2015

Spectral Imaging for Angiography and Diagnostic CT

Multi-Layer and Photon-Counting Detectors

We model spectral detector systems for material decomposition and quantitative CT, including photon-counting and multi-layer flat-panel detector designs. The work combines detector physics, cascaded-systems analysis, and task-based image-quality modeling to predict the bias, variance, and performance tradeoffs of spectral imaging systems.

  • Detector modeling for photon-counting and multi-layer detectors
  • Material decomposition algorithms and image-quality prediction
  • Theoretical modeling of spectral image quality
Selected Presentations
  • Theoretical prediction of bias in model-based material decomposition - SPIE Medical Imaging 2026

Spectral Digital Subtraction Angiography

Digital subtraction angiography is sensitive to motion between mask and contrast frames. We develop spectral-temporal processing methods that use energy-resolved measurements to improve vessel visibility, material separation, and robustness to motion in interventional angiography.

  • Joint temporal and spectral processing with photon-counting detectors
  • Motion-robust material separation for angiographic imaging
Selected Presentations
  • Three-material decomposition using a dual-layer flat-panel detector in the presence of soft tissue motion - SPIE Medical Imaging 2023

Deep Learning Image Formation

Assessment of Deep Learning Reconstruction

Deep learning reconstruction and post-processing methods are nonlinear and data dependent. We develop analysis and observer-study methods to evaluate how learned algorithms change image structure, local noise, and diagnostic interpretation, including how uncertainty and variability should be communicated to radiologists.

  • Analysis of deep learning reconstruction and denoising behavior
  • Observer studies for reconstruction uncertainty and variability display
Selected Presentations
  • Communicating variability in deep learning CT reconstruction to radiologists - Deep Reconstruction Workshop 2025

Image Synthesis with Radiomics Supervision

We develop domain-guided generative models for image formation and image-analysis evaluation. This work incorporates imaging physics and quantitative feature constraints into learned models, including radiomics-supervised synthesis of lung CT image content.

  • Radiomics-conditioned image synthesis
  • Domain knowledge incorporated into generative imaging models
Selected Presentations
  • A generative model of lung CT conditioned on radiomics features - SPIE Medical Imaging 2026

Physics-Guided Radiomics Standardization

Radiomics Feature Standardization

Radiomics features depend on spatial resolution, noise, dose, reconstruction kernel, and scanner configuration. We use prior knowledge of image blur and noise to predict scanner-dependent feature changes and recover more consistent feature values for downstream quantitative analysis.

  • Standardization of histogram- and texture-based radiomics features
  • Prediction and correction of scanner-dependent feature shifts from blur and noise
  • Evaluation of feature recovery in classification tasks
Selected Presentations
  • End-to-end modeling for predicting and estimating radiomics: application to GLCM in CT - SPIE Medical Imaging 2021

Image Quality & Task-Driven Imaging

Image Chain Modeling and Model Observers

We model the imaging chain from data acquisition through reconstruction and image interpretation. The goal is to connect system design choices to task performance, including objective and model-observer methods for subjective image-quality assessment.

  • Image-chain modeling for acquisition and reconstruction design
  • Model observers for image-quality assessment
Selected Presentations
  • Joint optimization of fluence field modulation and regularization for multi-task objectives - SPIE Medical Imaging 2018

Image Analysis

Automatic Working-Angle Prediction for Neuro-Interventional Procedures

We develop image-analysis and quantitative feature-extraction methods for procedural planning and evaluation. In neuro-interventional applications, this includes automatic working-angle prediction for intracranial aneurysm treatment using anatomical models derived from 3D angiographic imaging.

  • Segmentation and quantitative feature extraction from 3D angiography
  • Automatic neck-view and barrel-view prediction for aneurysm treatment
Selected Presentations
  • Automatic viewing angles prediction for endovascular treatment in intracranial aneurysm - SPIE Medical Imaging 2026
