Research

From blackboard to benchtop to bedside — we develop CT and cone-beam CT technologies grounded in a rigorous understanding of image science.

Theme 1

Novel Imaging Techniques for Interventional Applications

Non-Circular Orbits for Image Quality Improvement

Conventional circular scan orbits suffer from cone-beam artifacts and severe metal artifacts near implants and surgical hardware. We design and implement novel orbits — such as sawtooth and universal metal-tolerant trajectories — on clinical robotic C-arm systems (e.g., Siemens ARTIS pheno), including fully automatic online geometric calibration with arbitrarily placed fiducials.

  • Universal orbit design for metal artifact elimination
  • Practical workflows for arbitrary orbits on clinical robotic C-arms
  • Revealing pelvic and spinal structures near metal hardware
Selected Presentations
  • Non-circular CT orbit design for elimination of metal artifacts — SPIE Medical Imaging 2020 (paper + presentation recording)
  • Task-driven acquisition and reconstruction in cone-beam CT — SPIE Medical Imaging 2015 (1st place, Physics of Medical Imaging Student Paper Award)

Theme 2

Spectral Imaging for Angiography and Diagnostic CT

Multi-Layer and Photon-Counting Detectors

We design and optimize spectral detector hardware, including a triple-layer flat-panel detector enabling three-material decomposition (soft tissue, bone, iodine/gadolinium contrast), and study photon-counting CT systems with realistic models of charge sharing, pulse pileup, and nonuniform response.

  • Triple-layer flat-panel detector design optimization
  • Model-based and deep learning material decomposition
  • Multiple focal spots for high-resolution photon-counting CT

Spectral Digital Subtraction Angiography

Temporal subtraction in DSA is highly sensitive to patient motion. Our joint spectral-temporal processing strategy combines energy-resolved measurements across time frames, improving vessel visibility and motion robustness for interventional angiography.

  • Joint temporal and spectral processing with photon-counting detectors (IEEE TBME, 2025)
  • Diffusion posterior sampling for 4D-DSA from limited-angle data
  • High-sensitivity iodine imaging combining spectral CT technologies
Selected Presentations
  • Three-material decomposition using a dual-layer flat-panel detector in the presence of soft tissue motion — SPIE Medical Imaging 2023 (paper + presentation recording)

Theme 3

Deep Learning Image Formation

Image Quality Assessment of Deep Learning Algorithms

Deep learning reconstruction and denoising algorithms are nonlinear and data-dependent, so traditional image quality metrics do not directly apply. We develop assessment frameworks for these algorithms and study how to communicate their variability to radiologists.

  • Local linearity and Jacobian analysis of denoising networks
  • Communicating variability in deep learning CT reconstruction with posterior sampling

Diffusion Models and Generative Networks

We develop diffusion-based reconstruction methods conditioned on physically accurate, nonlinear measurement models. Because these methods sample from a posterior distribution, they also offer a principled way to quantify uncertainty and communicate it to radiologists. Related work uses generative networks to synthesize realistic lesions for algorithm development and evaluation.

  • Spectral diffusion posterior sampling (IEEE TBME Featured Article)
  • GANs with radiomics supervision for lung lesion generation
  • CTorch: GPU-accelerated differentiable projector toolbox
Selected Presentations
  • Generalized prediction framework for reconstructed image properties using neural networks — SPIE Medical Imaging 2019
  • Communicating Variability in Deep Learning CT Reconstruction to Radiologists (observer study) — Deep Reconstruction Workshop 2025, recording on YouTube

Theme 4

Physics-Guided Radiomics Standardization

Recovering Robust Quantitative Features

Radiomic features feed predictive models for diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment response — but they vary with scanner, dose, and reconstruction kernel. We derived a theoretical framework that models how blur and noise corrupt features such as gray-level co-occurrence matrices, and recover the underlying ground-truth values.

  • Standardization of histogram- and GLCM-based radiomics (PMB, 2021)
  • Theoretical prediction of feature variance to improve classification
  • Experimental validation on clinical scanners across dose and kernel
Selected Presentations
  • End-to-end modeling for predicting and estimating radiomics: application to GLCM in CT — SPIE Medical Imaging 2021 (paper + presentation recording)
  • When simulation becomes real: a 3D-printed power-law phantom in tomosynthesis imaging — SPIE IWBI 2024

Image Quality and Task-Based Assessment

A long-standing foundation of the lab is the quantitative assessment of imaging performance with respect to the clinical task. We have extended classical analysis frameworks to nonlinear and data-dependent algorithms, and validate predictions experimentally using 3D-printed, patient-specific phantoms developed with the PixelPrint collaboration.

  • Task-driven acquisition, fluence field modulation, and regularization design
  • Performance analysis for nonlinear tomographic data processing
  • Patient-specific 3D-printed CT phantoms for image quality assessment
Selected Presentations
  • Joint optimization of fluence field modulation and regularization for multi-task objectives — SPIE Medical Imaging 2018

Theme 5

Clinical Translation

Automatic View Angle Prediction for Aneurysm Treatment

During endovascular treatment of intracranial aneurysms, finding the optimal C-arm working angle is time-consuming and operator-dependent. We developed a neural network-based system that automatically predicts optimal viewing angles from pre-operative imaging, in collaboration with the Department of Neurosurgery at Penn and Siemens Healthineers.

  • First place, SPIE Early-Career Investigator Award 2026 (Ma et al.)
  • Siemens Healthineers-funded development toward clinical deployment
Selected Presentations
  • Automatic viewing angles prediction for endovascular treatment in intracranial aneurysm — SPIE Medical Imaging 2026 (1st place, Early-Career Investigator Award)

Flow & Feature Extraction

We develop quantitative analysis methods for vascular imaging that extract vessel geometry and flow information from DSA and CT, supporting diagnosis and treatment planning in neurovascular and hepatic applications.

  • 3D vessel segmentation of portal and hepatic veins
  • Centerline extraction and vessel geometry quantification from DSA
